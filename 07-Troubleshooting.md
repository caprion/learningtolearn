# Troubleshooting

Common challenges and what to do about them.

---

## "My child isn't engaged"

**Signs:** One-word answers, fidgeting, eyes glazing over.

**Likely cause:** The prompt is too abstract for their current energy, or the timing is wrong.

**Fixes:**
- Switch to something physical — a Maker's Lab build, or just go outside
- Try a different subject entirely (Math not landing? Try the Gossip prompt from English)
- Stop for today. A bored child learns nothing. Try again tomorrow with a different prompt.

**Remember:** You're looking for moments of genuine curiosity. You can't manufacture them — only create the conditions.

---

## "I don't know the answer to their question"

**Signs:** Child asks "But WHY does..." and you're stuck.

**This is the best outcome, not a failure.**

**Say this:** "Great question. I actually don't know. Let's figure it out together."

Then: Google it together, or write the question down and look it up later.

**Why this matters:** You're modelling that adults don't know everything, and that curiosity + finding out is more valuable than having answers ready.

---

## "The prompt isn't working"

**Signs:** Child is confused, the activity fizzles out after 30 seconds.

**Fixes:**
- Don't force it. Some prompts won't click with your child's personality or age
- Try a simpler version: if "Scale recipe from 3 to 8 guests" is too hard, try "What if 6 guests?" (exactly double — easier starting point)
- Rephrase in your own words. The prompts are templates, not scripts
- Move on. There are 50+ prompts — no need to make any single one work

---

## "I'm overwhelmed by the amount of content"

**Signs:** You've read everything and now feel like you should be doing all of it.

**Fix:** Ignore everything except the [Quick Reference](00-Quick-Reference.md).

Pick **one subject** based on what you're doing today. Pick **one prompt** from that subject. That's it.

**Target:** 3-4 prompts per week, not 3-4 per day. This is a slow drip, not a firehose.

---

## "My child wants to keep going but I'm tired"

**This is a great problem.** Let them run with it. You can step back and observe.

If they're deep into a Maker's Lab build or writing forged notes from Hagrid, your job is done — get out of the way.

---

## "It feels like school and my child is resisting"

**Likely cause:** You're pushing too hard, or the prompts feel like "tests."

**Fixes:**
- Never say "Let's do a learning nudge" — just ask the question naturally mid-conversation
- Drop the educational framing entirely. "How many wheels on this road?" doesn't need a preamble
- If your child senses homework, the nudge is dead. Retreat and try again another day, more casually

---

## The Golden Rule

**If it's not fun for both of you, stop.**

These are nudges, not obligations. The best learning happens when neither of you realizes it's "learning."
