# Geography

*Based on "Learning How to Learn" principles.*

**The Goal:** Move from Map Memorization to Spatial Connection.

---

## 🌍 Mode 1: The Zoom Out (Spatial Awareness)
*Target: Understanding scale and nested hierarchies.*

**The "Google Earth" Zoom**
"Close your eyes. Visualize your bed."
1.  Zoom out to the Building.
2.  Zoom out to the Street (What shops are nearby?).
3.  Zoom out to the City (Where is the Sea/River?).
4.  Zoom out to the State (Maharashtra/Karnataka...).
5.  Zoom out to India.
6.  Zoom out to Earth.

**The "Detective Journey" (Cardinal Directions)**
"If you are in [Current City] and you fly straight **South**, what happens?"
*   *Prompt:* "Which state do you hit next? Do you hit an ocean? If you keep going, do you hit Antarctica?"

---

## 🍛 Mode 2: The Breakfast Map (Trade & Agriculture)
*Target: Connecting daily life to geography.*

**The "Idli/Roti" Trace**
"Look at your breakfast (Idli/Dosa or Roti/Paratha)."
*   *Rice:* "Where does Rice grow? Does it need lots of water (Rain) or dry land? So did this come from a wet place (Kerala/Coastal) or a dry place?"
*   *Wheat:* "Where does Wheat grow? (Punjab/North). How did it get to our kitchen?"

**The "Clothes Tag" Hunt**
"Check the tag on your t-shirt. 'Made in...'? Find that country on the map. Is it far? How did the shirt get here? Ship or Plane?"

---

## 🗺️ Mode 3: The Map Maker (Representation)
*Target: Abstracting reality into symbols.*

**The "Bedroom Map"**
"Draw a map of your bedroom for a stranger. If "North" is the window, draw the bed and door."
*   *Check:* "Did you include the scale? Is the bed bigger than the chair?"

**The "Treasure Hunt"**
"Hide a coin in the living room. Draw a map with 'X marks the spot'. Give it to me. If I can find it using *only* the map, you win."

---

## 🌦️ Mode 4: The Weather Watcher (Climate)
*Target: Observation and prediction.*

**The "Rain Prediction"**
"Look at the sky. Will it rain today? Why?"
*   *Clues:* "Are the clouds fluffy (Cumulus) or flat and grey (Stratus)? Is the wind cool?"

**The "Shadow Clock"**
"Look at the shadow of that tree/pole. It is long or short? So what time is it roughly?" (Short = Noon, Long = Morning/Evening).
