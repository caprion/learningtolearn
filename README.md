# Learning Nudges

A parent's guide to sparking curiosity in 9-10 year olds through everyday moments.

No worksheets. No screen time. Just conversations that make your child *think* — while cooking, shopping, walking, or reading together.

**Philosophy:** Calm, low-friction, parent-led. Built on "Learning How to Learn" (Oakley) and "Toys from Trash" (Arvind Gupta). Prompts are indexed by *context* (kitchen, market, travel) not by curriculum topic — so you pick what fits the moment, not the syllabus.

---

## Your First Learning Nudge (Start Here)

**Right now, before reading further:**

1. What are you doing with your child today?
2. Open the **[Quick Reference](00-Quick-Reference.md)** — it's one page
3. Find a prompt that matches your context:
   - **Cooking?** → *Kitchen Chemist:* "This recipe feeds 3. We have 8 guests. Go get the spoons and show me the new ratios."
   - **In the car?** → *Traffic Jam:* "Are there more wheels or windows on this road right now? Don't count — estimate."
   - **Reading time?** → *Gossip Auntie:* "How would a nosy neighbor describe what [Character] just did?"
4. Read it aloud to your child
5. See what happens

**That's it. You just did a learning nudge.** Tomorrow, try a different one.

---

## What to Expect

- **Frequency:** 3-4 prompts per week (not per day!)
- **Duration:** 10-20 minutes each
- **Some will land, some won't.** That's normal. Skip and try another.
- **You don't need to know the "right" answer.** Figuring it out together is the point.
- **If it feels forced, stop.** These are nudges, not mandatory curriculum.

See [Troubleshooting](07-Troubleshooting.md) for common challenges and fixes.

## Why This Works

**Context-indexed, not curriculum-indexed.** You don't think "today I'll teach fractions." You think "we're cooking tonight" — and the prompt is right there.

**Dialogue, not instructions.** Every prompt is written so you can read it aloud. No preparation needed.

**Cross-linked.** The Cipher Wheel appears in both English and Maker's Lab. The Salt Map connects Geography to hands-on building. Subjects interleave naturally.

**Indian context throughout.** ₹500 notes, Wankhede Stadium, Idli vs Roti, Mumbai auto-rickshaws — your child recognizes the world in these prompts.
