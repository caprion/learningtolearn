# English Literature

*Based on "Learning How to Learn" principles.*

**Current Favorites:**
*   Roald Dahl (Matilda, BFG)
*   Harry Potter
*   Ruskin Bond & R.K. Narayan
*   Sudha Murty

---

## 🧠 Parent's Guide (The "Why")
*   **The "Gossip" Effect:** Humans are wired to remember social information (who did what to whom). Frame questions as "secrets" or "gossip."
*   **Procedural > Declarative:** Don't ask *about* the style. Ask them to *steal* the style (Procedural Learning).
*   **Indian Context:** Use the "Mumbai Transfer" to map abstract British/Fantasy concepts onto their daily reality (Malgudi/Traffic/Monsoon).

---

## 🎥 Mode 1: The Director's Cut (Visualization)
*Target: Turning words into mental movies (Diffuse Mode)*

**The "CCTV" Challenge**
"If you were a CCTV camera on the wall during [Scene], draw exactly what you saw. No words allowed first. Then add 3 captions."
*   *Harry Potter:* The mess in the Common Room.
*   *Roald Dahl:* The layout of the Chocolate Factory's specialized room.
*   *Ruskin Bond:* The view from the "Room on the Roof."

**The "Silent Movie"**
"Act out [Character's] reaction to the bad news without speaking. I will guess which sentence you are acting out."
*   *Matilda:* Act out being threatened by Miss Trunchbull (fear vs. hidden defiance).
*   *Swami:* Act out breaking the windowpane and fearing the Headmaster.

**The "Sensory Chef"**
"Describe the *smell* and *taste* of this scene. The book says 'delicious', but you tell me the ingredients."
*   *Enid Blyton:* Describe the midnight feast. (Is it just bread and jam, or magic?)
*   *Sudha Murty:* Describe the temple prasad.

---

## 🕵️‍♀️ Mode 2: The Desi Detective (Retrieval Practice)
*Target: Social cognition and active recall*

**The "Gossip Auntie/Uncle"**
"Imagine [Character] is a gossiping neighbor in your building. How would they explain what just happened to their friend on the phone? Use their voice!"
*   *Roald Dahl:* How would a neighbor describe George making his "Medicine" ("Arre, that boy is mixing paint and shampoo!")
*   *Wimpy Kid:* How would Greg's mom describe his latest disaster to her friend?

**The "Lie Detector"**
"I'm going to tell you three things about the chapter. Two are true, one is a lie. Catch me!"
*   *Tip:* Get small details wrong (e.g., "Swami wore a red coat" instead of "Malgudi Cricket Club cap"). Correcting the adult provides a dopamine hit.

**The "Unsaid" Investigation**
"The author has a 'Secret Camera' that hides things. Find a sentence where characters are thinking something but saying something else."

---

## 🎭 Mode 3: The Copycat Criminal (Style & Structure)
*Target: Chunking/Pattern Recognition*

**The "Forged Note"**
"You need to write a fake sick note from [Character] to the teacher/Principal. It has to sound EXACTLY like them."
*   *Hagrid:* Use 'yer', 'blimey', and mention a dragon.
*   *Geronimo Stilton:* Use *Italic fonts*, CHEESY metaphors, and exclamation marks!
*   *Captain Underpants:* Misspell words and add a "Flip-o-rama" drawing.

**The "Translation" Board**
"Translate this fancy sentence into:
1.  How *you* speak to your friends.
2.  How a Mumbai traffic cop would say it.
3.  How a strict news reader would say it."

**The "Indian Remix" (The Mumbai Transfer)**
"If this story happened in India..."
*   *Food:* Instead of Treacle Tart/Scones? (e.g., Gulab Jamun, Samosa)
*   *Travel:* Instead of the Knight Bus? (e.g., A magical Auto-Rickshaw).
*   *Creatures:* Instead of a fox? (e.g., A monkey or stray dog).

---

## 📊 Mode 4: The Analyst (Interleaving)
*Target: Mixing Math/Logic with Reading*

**The "Mood Graph"**
"Draw a line graph of [Character's] bravery from page 1 to 10. Why did it drop on page 7?"

**The "Inventory" (For Adventure Books)**
"List every item [Character] is carrying. Which one is most useless? Which one will save them?"
*   *Spy Books/Alex Rider/Adventure:* Essential for tracking plot devices.
